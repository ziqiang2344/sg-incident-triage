How it works

Each stage is cheaper than the next and passes on fewer frames.

Rank every frame

Signals that need no labels score each camera frame: how unlike it is to the same camera at other times, and how close CLIP puts it to descriptions of incidents. Road speed data can add a second, independent signal; it is not part of this test.

Check the top candidates with a VLM

A vision-language model sees the frame and the one before it, nothing else, and answers in a fixed format: incident or not, type, confidence, evidence. It may say it can't tell. It never sees the incident reports.

A person confirms

Labels are collected blind: the labelling page shows only the frames, in random order, with no scores or verdicts. Known incident reports are used to check what the ranking misses.

Limits

    Next: signalised junctions

    The public cameras watch expressways, which have no traffic lights. The same pipeline would run on junction CCTV, where the labels that matter are conflicts and near-misses, which are far more frequent than crashes.

    • Rank junction frames by unusual vehicle and pedestrian paths, check candidates with the VLM, confirm with a person.
    • Relate confirmed conflicts to signal timing using LTA's on-request Traffic Lights Traffic Plans and Indicative Traffic Counts at Junctions by Loop Detectors.
