Measuring.

How each measure is counted
  • Delay is a vehicle's travel time minus its time at its own desired speed, averaged over vehicles that finished their trip.
  • Stops count each time a vehicle comes to a halt after moving.
  • Rear-end conflict is a time-to-collision with the vehicle ahead under 1.5 s while closing faster than 3 m/s, a common threshold in surrogate safety studies.
  • Hard braking is a deceleration above 3.4 m/s², about a third of g.
  • Red-light run is a vehicle crossing the stop line after its amber has ended. Drivers decide at the start of amber whether they can stop in time.
  • Side-impact conflict is a main-road vehicle and a side-street vehicle inside the junction at the same time.

How this fits the proposal

  1. Calibrate. LTA's loop-detector counts and signal plans set the traffic and the timings at each junction.
  2. Label. The incident feed and camera footage mark where incidents and conflicts between vehicles happen. A VLM describes each candidate and a person confirms it.
  3. Learn. An RL agent per junction learns timings in the simulator, replacing the adaptive rule here. This semester at NUS I led a multi-agent RL project on air traffic that re-plans flights to avoid contrail regions while keeping complexity low. See its 3D replay.
  4. Test. Each candidate timing is scored on delay and collision risk in simulation before anything changes on the road.
